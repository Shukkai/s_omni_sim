# Unstructured KV pruning

*What an irregular mask costs that a compacted one does not*

Generated by `analysis/memory/unstructured_run.py` — re-run it to refresh.
Model: LLaMA-3-8B on Omni-LUT, 4-bit KV, context 32,768, 64 B DRAM burst, standard attention.
Every prior KV result was measured on a *compacted* retained set. This asks how much of that saving survives the mask being irregular.
Dense decode attention baseline: 3,221,323,776 B.

## Key findings

- **A 4-bit KV entry is 64 B, exactly one burst — so the layout decides everything.** In token-major, a *token* mask cuts between entries and is free at any granularity; a *channel* mask cuts inside one and is sub-burst. Channel-major inverts it exactly.
- **Unstructured channel pruning in token-major saves nothing at all.** 50% of channels removed, **0.0% of the traffic saved** — the read degrades to precisely dense. It is not a partial loss; the saving is gone.
- **The two axes want opposite layouts, so combining them is not possible without losing one.** At 50%+50% the best any single layout achieves is 0.50x of the joint saving, and the loss lands entirely on whichever axis the layout did not favour.
- **Head-wise is the only axis that is free in both layouts**, because a head is its own address region — and it is the one the pruning literature uses least.
- **Channel pruning is now null on *both* axes**: `study.md` §5 showed it does not move cycles, and this shows an unstructured mask does not move bytes either. Structured, entry-contiguous channel groups are the only version that pays.

## A. Why the layout decides — run length against the burst

A retained element is 4 bits. What matters is how many of them sit next to each other, against a 64 B burst.

| layout | mask axis | group | contiguous run | burst amplification | aligned? |
|:---|:---|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| token-major | token-wise | 1 | 64 B | 1x | yes |
| token-major | token-wise | 16 | 1,024 B | 1x | yes |
| token-major | token-wise | 128 | 8,192 B | 1x | yes |
| token-major | channel-wise | 1 | 1 B | 64x | no |
| token-major | channel-wise | 16 | 8 B | 8x | no |
| token-major | channel-wise | 128 | 64 B | 1x | yes |
| channel-major | token-wise | 1 | 1 B | 64x | no |
| channel-major | token-wise | 16 | 8 B | 8x | no |
| channel-major | token-wise | 128 | 64 B | 1x | yes |
| channel-major | channel-wise | 1 | 16,384 B | 1x | yes |
| channel-major | channel-wise | 16 | 262,144 B | 1x | yes |
| channel-major | channel-wise | 128 | 2,097,152 B | 1x | yes |

> A token-major entry is `128 x 4/8` = **64 B, exactly one burst**. That single coincidence is the whole result: a token mask lands on entry boundaries and is aligned at *every* group size, down to gathering one isolated token. A channel mask cuts inside the entry, so its run is `group x 0.5 B` and needs the **full 128 channels** to reach one burst — meaning any channel pruning at all is sub-burst in this layout.

> A run is floored at 1 B in both the model and this table, so a single 4-bit element reads as 1 B / 64x rather than its true 0.5 B / 128x. That understates the finest-grained scatter, which is the conservative direction — and the clamp in section B makes it moot, since anything at or below one burst prices the same.

## B. Token-major: what channel-group granularity buys

50% of channels retained, varying how many of them are contiguous. Group 128 = the whole entry = compacted, which is what ThinK and every prior measurement assumed.

| channel group | run | logical | effective | vs dense | saving kept |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.5 B | 1,610,661,888 | 3,221,323,776 | 1.000x | 0.0% |
| 2 | 1 B | 1,610,661,888 | 3,221,323,776 | 1.000x | 0.0% |
| 4 | 2 B | 1,610,661,888 | 3,221,323,776 | 1.000x | 0.0% |
| 8 | 4 B | 1,610,661,888 | 3,221,323,776 | 1.000x | 0.0% |
| 16 | 8 B | 1,610,661,888 | 3,221,323,776 | 1.000x | 0.0% |
| 32 | 16 B | 1,610,661,888 | 3,221,323,776 | 1.000x | 0.0% |
| 64 | 32 B | 1,610,661,888 | 3,221,323,776 | 1.000x | 0.0% |
| 128 | 64 B | 1,610,661,888 | 1,610,661,888 | 0.500x | 100.0% |

> **It is a cliff, not a slope.** Every group below 128 gives the same answer — zero — because any sub-burst run is rounded to the same 64 B, and the clamp then stops the charge exceeding a dense read. There is no partial credit for a *partly* structured channel mask: 64 contiguous channels out of 128 is worth exactly as much as one, which is nothing. Only the full entry pays.

## C. The layout conflict

Both axes at 50%, fully unstructured (group 1), measured under each layout. This is the table the study exists for.

| layout | mask | logical | effective | saving kept |
|:---|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| token-major | token-wise 50% | 0.500x | 0.500x | 100.0% |
| token-major | channel-wise 50% | 0.500x | 1.000x | 0.0% |
| channel-major | token-wise 50% | 0.500x | 1.000x | 0.0% |
| channel-major | channel-wise 50% | 0.500x | 0.501x | 99.9% |

> **Perfectly antisymmetric.** Each layout makes one axis free and the other worthless, and there is no third layout: a KV element has two indices, so one of them is the minor axis and the other is strided. Choosing a layout is therefore choosing *which pruning axis is allowed to work* — a decision made in the memory subsystem that silently determines which pruning papers can be deployed at all.

## D. Combining axes — head + token + channel

Token-major. Head-wise drops KV heads, which is contiguous in either layout. Each row adds one axis on top of the row above.

| mask | logical | effective | TPOT | speedup |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| dense | 1.000x | 1.000x | 127.49 ms | 1.000x |
| head 50% | 0.500x | 0.500x | 120.94 ms | 1.054x |
| head + token 50% | 0.250x | 0.250x | 84.76 ms | 1.504x |
| head + token + channel 50% | 0.125x | 0.250x | 84.76 ms | 1.504x |
| head + token + channel (chan grp 128) | 0.125x | 0.125x | 83.45 ms | 1.528x |

> The third axis is where it breaks. Head and token compose cleanly — both are entry-aligned — but adding an unstructured channel mask removes a further 50% logically and **moves the effective traffic not at all**. The last row is the same mask with contiguous 128-channel groups, i.e. structured, and it is the only version that converts.

## E. What the mask itself costs to store

An unstructured mask has to be written down somewhere and re-read to drive the gather. Token-major, 50%/50%, context 32,768.

| mask_mode | what it stores | effective | vs dense |
|:---|:---|---:|---:|
| none | not charged (what prior studies assumed) | 805,322,752 | 0.250x |
| static | one token + one channel bitmap per head | 808,484,864 | 0.251x |
| per_token | one bit per (token, channel) element | 1,207,988,224 | 0.375x |

> A per-element mask is **1 bit against a 4-bit datum — 25% of the dense cache**, charged over the full context whether or not the element survived. That is the structural cost of the finest-grained mask, and it lands on top of the granularity penalty rather than instead of it. A per-head static mask is negligible by comparison and is what a deployable design would use.

## F. Does any of it reach token latency?

Token-major, fully unstructured masks, at both batch extremes. DRAM share of decode grows with batch, so this is where granularity has its best chance of mattering.

**batch 1**

| mask | qk cycles | attn_v cycles | attn DRAM | TPOT | speedup |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| token 50% | 2,580,480 | 50,466,816 | 0.500x | 88.70 ms | 1.437x |
| channel 50% (unstructured) | 2,875,392 | 100,798,464 | 1.000x | 127.49 ms | 1.000x |
| channel 50% (compacted) | 2,875,392 | 100,798,464 | 0.500x | 122.25 ms | 1.043x |

**batch 32**

| mask | qk cycles | attn_v cycles | attn DRAM | TPOT | speedup |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| token 50% | 82,575,360 | 1,614,938,112 | 0.500x | 1326.44 ms | 1.936x |
| channel 50% (unstructured) | 92,012,544 | 3,225,550,848 | 1.000x | 2567.96 ms | 1.000x |
| channel 50% (compacted) | 92,012,544 | 3,225,550,848 | 0.500x | 2400.19 ms | 1.070x |

> **Token pruning is the only axis that works on both counts** — it cuts `kv_len`, which is the `K` of both attention GEMMs, so cycles fall *and* its bytes are entry-aligned. Channel pruning cuts `N` of `attn_v`, which the `LUT_OS_V` round cost has no term for, so cycles do not move; unstructured, its bytes do not move either, and the speedup is exactly 1.000x. **Two independent nulls on the same axis.**

## G. What this does and does not say

> **Accuracy is out of scope.** Unstructured masks exist because they are more accurate at equal budget. This prices that choice; it does not dispute it. The finding is that the price is paid in full and the benefit is not collected — which is an argument for structured masks, not against pruning.

> **The clamp is doing real work and should be understood.** A scattered read is never charged more than reading the whole region it sits inside, because a controller would simply do that instead. Without it the model reports up to 128x amplification, which is arithmetic rather than a cost. With it, the failure mode is stated correctly: the saving goes to **zero**, not negative.

> **Two things are modelled optimistically.** Gather *scheduling* is free here — no request-queue or MSHR pressure, though a 128-way scattered gather generates far more outstanding requests than a streaming read. And the mask is assumed already resident when the gather issues, so its read latency never serialises against the KV read. Both push in the same direction: **unstructured masks are worse than this report says, not better.**

> **Bit-width would change the entry size and therefore the whole table.** At 4-bit the entry is exactly one burst; at 8-bit it is two, and at 3-bit it is 48 B and aligned with nothing (`selective_report.md` B). The alignment that makes token pruning free is a property of this configuration, not a law.
