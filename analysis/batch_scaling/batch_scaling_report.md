# KV reduction vs batch: were we measuring in the right place?

Generated by `analysis/batch_scaling/batch_run.py`.
Model: LLaMA-3-8B on Omni-LUT, 4-bit KV.  See the module
docstring for why batch is the axis that matters here.

  Weight traffic is constant in batch -- read once, reused across the
  batch -- while KV scales linearly.  'max speedup' is what a technique
  that removed *all* KV traffic could achieve on decode DRAM.

  Fraction of dense decode DRAM remaining.  At batch 1 even aggressive

  are most of it.

D. Two different stories, and they must not be conflated

  ENTRY-COUNT techniques (eviction, selection) were measured in the
  wrong place.  They cut kv_len, so their saving is pure DRAM traffic,
  and traffic is exactly what batch makes dominant.  evict-1024 at 32K
  goes 2.46x -> 15.96x from batch 1 to 32; select-3% goes 2.40x ->
  12.85x.  Their batch-1 numbers understate them several-fold.

  CHANNEL pruning (ThinK) does NOT recover: 1.034x -> 1.054x at 32K.
  This is not the same ceiling.  ThinK does cut bytes -- section C shows
  decode DRAM falling to 0.825x at batch 32 -- but latency barely moves,
  so the bytes it saves were not on the critical path.  attn_v is
  compute-bound under a 4-bit KV cache, and the LUT_OS_V round cost has
  no N term, so pruning head_dim idles array columns instead of saving
  cycles.  That is the cycle null from section 5, and it is a property
  of the dataflow, not of the measurement point.  More batch cannot fix
  it; a different dataflow or head-packing might.

  So: re-baseline eviction and selection at the batch the accelerator is
  actually meant to serve.  Leave section 5's ThinK conclusion standing --
  it was right, for the reason it gave.
